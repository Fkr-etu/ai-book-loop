# Product Pricing Strategy

## Purpose

This document defines the current pricing hypothesis for Book Loop. It is not a guarantee of future prices or evidence that willingness to pay has been validated.

Customers pay for a reliable workflow that protects narrative coherence, not for raw tokens or access to a particular LLM.

## Current working launch grid

Keep the public model simple while the Book wedge is being validated:

| Plan | Monthly | Annual | Commercial role |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Free | €0 | — | Product discovery |
| Creator | €19 | €190 | Serious individual creator |
| Pro | €39 | €390 | Intensive creator / multiple projects |

The capacity policy defines the corresponding active-project and workflow-run envelopes. The product must not promise unlimited AI generation.

There is currently no Studio tier in the implemented commercial model. A higher-capacity tier may be tested later if usage evidence justifies it.

## Plan intent

### Free — €0

The free plan exists to let a creator experience the complete Book Loop concept with bounded usage. It should demonstrate the loop rather than merely provide a crippled editor.

### Creator — €19/month or €190/year

Target: an author actively producing or revising a book or series. The plan provides the core Book Loop workflow, Canon/continuity checks, version and review history, and a meaningful but bounded workflow allowance.

### Pro — €39/month or €390/year

Target: creators with higher workflow demand or multiple active projects. Capacity is increased without changing the core value proposition.

## Pricing principles

1. Price the outcome, not the model.
2. Keep the public model understandable.
3. Use internal quotas to control inference cost.
4. Do not promise unlimited premium-model inference.
5. Do not introduce a complex credit economy before users understand the product.
6. Validate pricing through paid usage and retention, not competitor imitation.
7. Adjust capacity from measured economics before assuming that higher prices are the answer to expensive workloads.

## What remains a hypothesis

The €19 / €39 grid is the current working commercial configuration because it is already reflected by the billing/capacity model and current product surfaces. It is not yet validated by sufficient real-customer evidence.

The validation program should measure:

- free-to-paid conversion;
- retention and churn;
- completed workflows per paying creator;
- percentage of users hitting capacity limits;
- LLM cost per workflow and per paying creator;
- gross margin after payment processing and infrastructure;
- upgrade/downgrade behavior;
- repeated use across chapters and revisions;
- willingness to pay at the current price points.

## Do not publish unsupported claims

Do not advertise a free trial unless the corresponding trial and billing lifecycle exists. Do not describe future collaboration, enterprise features or a future Studio tier as included in the current plans.

## Decision gates

Pricing becomes a stable commercial decision only when:

1. creators understand the continuity value;
2. users return for repeated chapters/revisions;
3. users identify a meaningful advantage over a generic LLM plus existing tools;
4. real creators pay or show strong purchase intent;
5. measured usage supports healthy margins.

Until then, €19 / €39 is the working hypothesis, not a permanent commitment.
